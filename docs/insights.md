# Key Insights

### 1. Higher advertising investment is strongly associated with increased lead generation.

The analysis revealed a **strong positive correlation (r ≈ 0.79)** between monthly advertising spend and generated leads. Campaigns with larger marketing budgets consistently produced more leads, confirming that advertising investment remains an important driver of business growth.

---

### 2. Marketing profitability remained positive despite rising acquisition costs.

Overall **ROMI remained above 1.0** throughout the analyzed period, indicating that marketing campaigns continued to generate more value than they cost. While profitability fluctuated slightly, both Facebook and Google Ads maintained relatively stable returns on investment.

---

### 3. Customer acquisition became progressively more expensive.

Although lead generation increased alongside advertising spend, **Cost per Lead (CPL)** rose substantially during the analyzed period. This suggests diminishing acquisition efficiency, meaning that each additional lead required increasingly higher marketing investment.

---

### 4. User engagement weakened over time while advertising costs increased.

CTR gradually declined, indicating that a smaller share of users clicked on advertisements despite being exposed to them. At the same time, both **CPC** and **CPM** increased, meaning advertisers paid more both to reach audiences and to generate clicks.

Together, these trends suggest growing competition in advertising auctions or decreasing audience responsiveness.

---

### 5. Conversion efficiency after the click remained relatively stable.

Despite higher acquisition costs and declining CTR, **Clicks-to-Leads Conversion** remained stable and even improved slightly toward the end of the period. This indicates that once users engaged with advertisements, the landing pages or conversion process continued to perform efficiently.

---

### 6. Campaign performance differs significantly across marketing objectives.

Campaign rankings revealed substantial differences depending on the selected KPI.

For example:

* **Discounts** achieved the highest CTR while maintaining one of the lowest CPC values.
* **Brand** campaigns generated the highest Cost per Lead, suggesting that awareness-focused campaigns are considerably more expensive for direct lead acquisition.
* Campaign effectiveness varied considerably across ROMI, CPC, CPL, CTR, and conversion metrics, emphasizing the importance of evaluating campaigns using multiple business KPIs rather than relying on a single performance indicator.

---

### 7. Interactive KPI analysis enables multidimensional campaign evaluation.

The dashboard allows stakeholders to dynamically switch between key marketing metrics (CTR, CPC, CPM, CPL, ROMI, Click-to-Leads Conversion, and Reach-to-Leads Conversion), providing different perspectives on campaign effectiveness and supporting more informed optimization decisions.

---

The indicators form a logical chain:

**Spend ↑ → Leads ↑ (r≈0.79) → CTR ↓ → CPC & CPM ↑ → CPL ↑ → ROMI still > 1 → Conversion remains stable.**
